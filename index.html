Reading these numbers — a few honest caveats.
• A poll measures opinion on the day it was taken, not on election day — so the campaign average mixes real opinion change with pollster error. In long cycles (2021's polling window spans a full year) much of everyone's "error" is simply that opinion moved; Final month and Last poll are fairer tests of a firm's closing call.
• Firms stop polling at different distances from the finish (publishing polls is banned in the last days of the campaign), so "last polls" differ in age — the ranking shows how many days before the election each was taken.
• Seats are a blunt yardstick: the 3.25% threshold and seat allocation can turn a small vote-share miss into several seats, and per-list errors are not independent — seats sum to 120, so over-counting one list forces under-counting another.
• These are point estimates with no error bars. A firm rated on a handful of polls can rank high or low by luck; treat gaps of a few tenths of a seat as a tie.
• The all-cycles ranking uses a skill score — each firm's error divided by that election's field-average error (1× = typical, lower = better) — because some elections were much harder to poll than others, and firms took part in different ones.
• Under All polled, only lists that actually stood in the election are graded as threshold calls; lists that merged or withdrew mid-campaign are not counted as misses.
